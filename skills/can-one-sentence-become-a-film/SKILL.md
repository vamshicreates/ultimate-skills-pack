# Can one sentence become a film?

When the user names a film for this series, return one ready-to-record English script without asking for a brief. Treat the named film as an illustration of a compelling one-sentence story, not as proof that the film was originally pitched or written in that exact sentence. If the user gives a different language, runtime, or CTA, follow that request.

## Script shape

Target 30–45 seconds of natural speech, usually 75–100 words including the CTA. Keep sentences short and conversational. Use these three beats:

1. **Hook:** Open with the exact spoken question, “Can one sentence become a film?”
2. **Body:** Name the film. Give a fresh, accurate *one-sentence paraphrase* of its central character or force, goal, conflict, and stakes. Then briefly show how that sentence suggests scenes, choices, or emotion. Bridge to the viewer's own film idea. Use a distinctive observation for each film instead of repeating the same generic explanation.
3. **CTA:** End with the My Logline sign-up and comment request: “Sign up for My Logline and start writing your film. Comment ‘mylogline’ to get the link in your DM.” The closing can be split between voiceover and on-screen text, but both actions must be clear.

Return the script with **VO** and **CTA (VO and on-screen)** labels. The spoken script should be usable as written; keep production notes out unless the user asks for them. Do not add unrelated marketing copy, hashtags, or an extra explanation after the CTA.

## Accuracy and creative boundaries

- Check the film's premise when uncertain. Preserve its actual central conflict and outcome uncertainty; do not invent plot points to sharpen the hook.
- Paraphrase in original words. Do not use film dialogue or present the one-sentence summary as an official logline or a quote.
- Let the example answer the opening question by showing the story engine, while avoiding a claim that a single sentence alone made the finished film.
- If a film has several versions with materially different stories and the user's reference is ambiguous, use the best-known version and identify its year, or ask which one if that choice would substantially change the script.

## Quality check

Before sending, read the voiceover once for timing and flow. The film sentence should create a clear choice or tension, the body should make the writing lesson obvious without sounding like a lecture, and the final line should include both the My Logline sign-up and the exact comment keyword **mylogline**.
