# Coverage deliverables

Scale detail to the user's request and script length. Use clear prose with tables where they help comparison. A requested quick assessment still needs accurate scope and evidence; do not imply a complete reading when only an excerpt was reviewed.

Each evaluation must be delivered as an individual, locally stored PDF plus its editable Markdown source. Follow [local-pdf-output.md](local-pdf-output.md). This applies to single scripts, every script in a batch, revised-draft reviews, and limited assessments of partial/unreadable inputs. A batch summary supplements these files; it never replaces them.

## Individual script report

1. **Identity and scope:** Title, source/draft, language, genre, format, evaluation unit, target runtime or qualified estimate/range with timing basis, page/word count where useful, standalone/serialized context, intended audience/effect, date, and actual ranges read. For episodes, include series/season and episode identity/order. State the length-appropriate expectations applied and any missing context. Give a short original synopsis, including the ending when full coverage is intended. Clearly distinguish source page/scene locators from the report PDF's page numbers.
2. **Assessment:** Lead with the main judgment about this draft and its most consequential strength and weakness. Include the total out of 100 only when eligible, plus rubric ID and overall confidence. Do not present an incomplete assessment as a scored complete screenplay.
3. **Scorecard:** Eight rows showing category, weight, score out of 10 or unassessed, confidence, and a concise evidence-backed rationale. Show the calculation convention. A brief main table may link to fuller findings.
4. **What works:** Concrete strengths with source locators and why they matter. Identify elements that revision should protect.
5. **What limits the draft:** Prioritized findings with source evidence, the dramatic mechanism, likely reader effect, and alternative interpretations where material. Include emotional preparation/payoff, scene function, character choices, and dialogue as relevant; do not manufacture a weakness in every category.
6. **Emotional reading:** Trace pivotal changes using the emotional evidence ledger. Distinguish the author's intended effect, your predicted response, and actual human feedback if provided.
7. **Top three revision priorities:** For each, state the problem, proposed intervention, affected scenes, expected benefit, tradeoff, and a reader question or observable change that would help assess it. Use fewer priorities when fewer meaningful issues exist. Separate essential structural repairs from optional refinements.
8. **Revision potential:** Qualitative assessment of the promise and effort involved, independent of the current score. Do not award a speculative post-rewrite score.
9. **Optional reference comparison:** Include only when relevant, with source/scene evidence and limits on the analogy.
10. **Remaining uncertainty:** Specific missing text, uncertain cultural/linguistic readings, or questions for a native reader/table read. Keep this practical rather than generic.

Use compact scene notes for local issues. Do not substitute an exhaustive list of minor wording edits for the central diagnosis. Preserve the user's chosen report language and level of detail.

For micro-dramas, include a concise account of the central tension, emotional turn or progression, ending/serial handoff, and whether the action plausibly fits the intended length. For episodes, distinguish local movement from ongoing-series development. For multi-episode reviews, add a season/series synthesis PDF addressing continuity, progression, story engine, and cumulative payoffs; label whether the complete unit was read and keep its score separate from episode scores. Follow [format-and-length.md](format-and-length.md).

## Batch comparison

Produce individual reports plus a summary table with:

| Group / rank or status | Script / draft | Format / unit / runtime | Language / genre | Craft score | Confidence | Main strength | Main weakness | Revision potential |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Include every supplied candidate, with unranked reasons for unreadable, partial, or low-confidence cases. Exclude reference scripts from candidate ranks unless the user explicitly includes them. Label exact ties and close calls. Use the common rubric for cross-genre ranking and add genre-specific observations where useful.

Explain the leading scripts' decisive tradeoffs using source evidence. Name the strongest current draft, emotional execution, characters, and originality when those distinctions are useful and supported; do not invent an award for every entry. Identify the most promising revision separately. If no defensible ordering exists, say so rather than forcing one.

## Revised draft comparison

Show old and new category scores under the same rubric, then connect meaningful changes to actual changes in the text. Describe regressions and qualities preserved. Where source scope or understanding changed, disclose it rather than treating the score delta as a clean measurement of improvement.
