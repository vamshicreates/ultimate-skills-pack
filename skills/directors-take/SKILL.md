# Director's Take

Write one ready-to-record social video script when the user names a director and film for this series. If they name only a film, identify its director. If they name only a director, select a film with a clear, well-supported creative choice when the context makes that useful; otherwise ask which film they mean. Follow any explicit change to language, runtime, or CTA.

The series appreciates the daring decision that makes a film work. It does not claim to know a director's private thoughts. When the origin of an idea is documented, tell it accurately and credit the person who supplied the seed. The director's contribution may be the choice to develop, stage, or sustain that idea.

## Research

- Verify the film's premise, director, and any origin story before stating them. Prefer the director's own interview, official production material, or a firsthand collaborator interview. If the origin is undocumented, omit it and focus on an observable choice in the finished film.
- Distinguish a documented fact from the script's interpretation of why a choice works. Do not invent quotes, motivations, production struggles, or claims that someone was first to do something.
- Keep research links outside the spoken script in a short **Source note** when factual behind-the-scenes information appears.

## Script shape

Aim for **30–45 seconds**, usually **85–110 spoken English words** including the CTA. Preserve the progression of the [approved *Eega* example](references/approved-eega-script.md), while making the insight specific to each film:

1. **Director question:** Open with a sharp question about the director and film, often “What was [director] thinking when they made [film]?”
2. **Wild premise and seed:** State the surprising premise in one clean sentence. Add one verified origin detail if it strengthens the story.
3. **Creative leap:** Name the decision that made the unlikely premise emotionally convincing. Show its effect on the audience with concrete, punchy language.
4. **Takeaway and invitation:** Sum up the director's leap, then ask the viewer about their own wild film idea.
5. **CTA:** End with: “Sign up for My Logline and start writing your film. Comment ‘mylogline’ to get the link in your DM.”

Return a title with the film, then **VO** and **CTA — VO and on-screen**. Put any Source note below the script. Do not add shot lists, hashtags, or an explanation of the structure unless asked. Read the script for a seamless spoken flow; avoid a list of disconnected facts or a lecture on screenwriting.
