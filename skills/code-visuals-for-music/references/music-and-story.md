# Song meaning, timing, and story

## Decode the source

Probe the exact version: live, album, radio, and video edits can have different timing. Preserve source audio unless editing is requested. Derive audio features from a temporary working copy, not by modifying the source.

Measure energy and transients for accent candidates. Estimate tempo and downbeats, then verify against representative passages. Speech, rubato, pickups, half-time sections, and changing meters can break a regular grid. Keep important manually verified cues even if a detector disagrees.

ASR output is a draft. Listen to uncertain lines, compare alternate passes where useful, and use a user-supplied lyric sheet where available. Separate:

- `source_text`: original wording/script.
- `text`: the approved display form, such as Telugu in Latin letters.
- `meaning`: interpretation for storyboarding, not a replacement caption.
- `verified`: whether the wording and timing were checked.

Do not guess proper nouns or dialogue just to fill a cue. Keep uncertain cues marked and visible in the review. If the user requested all lyrics, full verified coverage is a separate completion criterion.

## Translate meaning into events

A visual metaphor must create an observable action. “Longing” can become an attempted connection that changes the space between people. “Obstacles” require specific resistance and a consequence: a bridge closes, a path loops, a character makes a different choice. Decorative geometry near a runner may not communicate a hurdle.

For each shot record:

1. What the viewer must understand first.
2. What the character wants and does.
3. What changes because of that action.
4. How the shot connects to the next one.
5. Which musical event motivates its timing.

Use emotional color changes purposefully: attraction, doubt, separation, pursuit, recognition. These are choices for a particular story, not fixed universal meanings for individual colors.

## Typography and dialogue

Select font size from actual viewing tests. Minimal can still mean readable; do not hardcode a tiny size across aspect ratios. Use contrast, quiet placement, restrained tracking, and enough hold time. Keep the text's entrance shorter than its reading period.

Distinguish sung captions from conversation. For a woman speaking on a phone with dialogue to her left, create that spatial relationship explicitly and test the text against her silhouette, hair, handset, and listener. A dialogue portrait should act through gaze, breath, pauses, brows, and gesture. Mouth motion alone is insufficient.

For lip-sync, align phonemes/visemes if an appropriate backend is available. If using amplitude to open a mouth, label the result approximate. Unverified transcription and approximate lip-sync are different limitations.
