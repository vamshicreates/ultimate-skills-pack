# Nine-cell storyboard guidelines

## Deliverable

Each finished scene receives one 3x3 storyboard sheet containing nine ordered visual cells. Internal source images may be generated separately, but individual cells are supporting material rather than required deliverables.

## Beat selection

Read the entire scene before selecting images. The nine cells must communicate the scene's geography, participants, progression, decisive moment, and exit state. Do not generate nine arbitrary attractive frames.

Use shot variation intentionally:

- wide shots establish geography and blocking;
- medium shots show relationships and action;
- close-ups emphasize decisions and reactions;
- inserts identify story-critical props or evidence;
- POV or over-the-shoulder views clarify perception and conflict; and
- the final cell should communicate the scene's ending condition or transition.

Do not invent an event, character, prop, injury, stunt, or location feature merely to fill a cell.

## Continuity controls

Before generation, lock:

- character facial structure, age range, body type, hair, and distinguishing traits;
- costume and costume condition for the scene;
- makeup, injuries, blood, dirt, and wetness;
- location layout, entrances, windows, key furniture, and screen geography;
- hero-prop appearance and ownership;
- vehicle color and model class;
- story time, weather, lighting direction, and palette; and
- aspect ratio and visual medium.

Reuse the same canonical references across connected scenes. When a visual detail is not established by the script or user, choose a coherent interpretation and record it as an interpretation.

## Sheet construction

- Use a landscape master canvas unless the user requests another format.
- Keep three equal columns and three equal rows with visible gutters.
- Preserve each source image without distorting faces or cropping away essential action.
- Add cell numbers outside the artwork.
- Keep captions short: shot type plus the key action or beat.
- Do not ask the image model to render dialogue, department notes, or long captions.
- Keep text large enough to read at the intended document size.

## Visual review

Inspect every cell for:

- correct characters and count;
- stable identity, costume, and physical condition;
- intended action and emotional beat;
- correct location and time of day;
- prop and vehicle continuity;
- coherent screen direction and geography;
- absence of duplicated limbs, broken anatomy, illegible faces, or accidental text;
- correct cell order; and
- consistent art direction.

Correct only the failing cell or region when possible. If a mismatch remains, disclose it instead of presenting the sheet as fully approved.

## Relationship to the breakdown

The storyboard is a visual interpretation, while the breakdown is the production record. The image must not introduce an unlisted production requirement without adding it to the breakdown as an inference or question.
